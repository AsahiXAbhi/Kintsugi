Our Philosophy

The art of imperfection

In Japanese, kintsugi means "golden joinery" — the practice of repairing broken pottery with gold, making the fracture more beautiful than the original. Our omakase follows the same principle: every piece of fish carries its own story of tide, season, and craft.

Chef Akira Tanabe trained for seven years at a two-Michelin-starred counter in Tokyo before bringing his practice to Vancouver. He sources directly from the Tsukiji successor market and local BC waters.

"I do not cook for the guest. I cook for the fish — to let it speak its truth."

— Chef Akira Tanabe

The Omakase

Tonight's journey

Every evening is different. What follows is a snapshot of a recent service — the actual courses shift with what arrives from the market each morning.

Nigiri Omakase

$185 per guest

Eight pieces of hand-pressed nigiri, each seasoned differently. Shari at body temperature, topped with seasonal fish aged according to Edomae tradition — from nimoto-cured saba to three-day aged hon-maguro.

18 courses ~2.5 hours Sake pairing +$95

Sashimi & Sake Flight

$120 per guest

Five cuts of sashimi sourced from the morning's auction, paired with a curated flight of five sakes. A lighter introduction to Chef Tanabe's craft, ideal for first-time visitors.

10 courses ~1.5 hours Wine pairing available

Dietary requirements — Please inform us of any allergies or dietary restrictions when booking. Chef Tanabe adapts each course individually.
